# Primer Template

Two shapes: the **Tier 0 map** (the default deliverable) and a **Tier 1
drill-down** (returned when the user picks a node). Omit empty optional lines
rather than filling them with generic prose.

## Tier 0 — The Map

```markdown
# [Topic]: Primer

> **In one paragraph:** [Three or four sentences: what it is, why it matters, and
> the single most important thing to understand. Someone who reads only this can
> hold a basic, non-embarrassing conversation.]

**What it is.** [One or two sentences. Define the term precisely; note any
contested definition.]

**Why it matters now.** [One or two sentences. The stakes, or the reason the topic
is live.]

**What you need to know**
1. **[Concept or fact].** [One or two sentences. Link the key source.]
2. **[Concept or fact].** [...]
3. **[...].** [Three to six items total.]

**The live debates**
- **[Tension].** [Where informed people disagree, and the axis of disagreement.
  Give the strongest form of each side.]
- **[Tension].** [...]

**Who or what matters** [players, institutions, works, or moving parts — include
only when the topic has them]
- **[Name]** — [one-line role].

**Vocabulary** [only the terms needed to follow the topic]
- **[Term]** — [plain definition].

**State of play.** [Current status and, where useful, the frontier or direction of
travel. Label settled vs contested vs speculative.]

## Go deeper

Pick a node and I will expand it with sources and nuance:
1. **[Node]** — [what a drill-down would add].
2. **[Node]** — [...].
3. **[Node]** — [...].

[Where depth belongs to a different kind of task, name it instead of over-reaching
— include only the lines that apply:]
- To test or defend a contested claim from above → a claim-testing pass.
- If it is really a legal or regulatory question → dedicated legal research.
- If it is a company or investment question → dedicated investment analysis.
- For current community sentiment → a recent-community scan.

**Confidence:** [High / Medium / Low overall.] [One line on the biggest
uncertainty or the largest gap in what could be verified.]
```

## Tier 1 — Drill-down

Returned when the user picks a node. Expand only that node; do not re-run the map.

```markdown
## Deeper: [Node]

[Two to four sentences of orientation on this node specifically.]

### [Load-bearing example or finding]

**Result:** [Exact datum and date, with link.]  
**Conditions:** [Variables needed to interpret it.]  
**Practical meaning:** [Plain-language translation or calculation.]  
**Limitation:** [Strongest reason not to overgeneralise.]  
**Confidence:** [High / Medium / Low and why.]

[Add a second card only when it changes the picture.]

**Still open:** [What this node does not settle.]
**Next:** [The next node worth drilling, or the specialist task if depth now
belongs elsewhere.]
```

## Information budget

- Keep the one-paragraph orientation under 90 words.
- Use three to six items in "What you need to know". Fewer is fine; do not pad.
- Keep the whole Tier 0 map to roughly 500-800 words.
- Keep a Tier 1 drill-down to roughly 200-400 words per node.
- Include "Who or what matters", "Vocabulary", and "Common misconceptions" only
  when they orient the reader. Drop any that would be filler.
- Link the source at the first claim it supports. Do not repeat a source list at
  the end.
- Keep every drill-down evidence card complete: result, conditions, practical
  meaning, limitation, and confidence. Remove the card rather than dropping the
  fields needed to interpret it.

## Compression test

Before delivering, confirm that a reader who sees only the one-paragraph
orientation and the bold labels can state what the topic is, why it matters, and
where the real disagreements lie. Confirm that every number in the map is
supported by an opened source and carries a date. Confirm that settled fact,
contested claim, and forecast are visibly distinguished. Remove any detail that
does not improve orientation, recall, or the reader's ability to ask a better
next question.
